## Course Overview

This undergraduate course examines the role of the United Nations in the promotion and protection of human rights across the globe.

It introduces students to the historical foundations, missions, objectives, and institutional framework of the United Nations. The course also explores the principal areas and issues in which the United Nations has intervened to improve the social, political, and economic conditions of individuals and communities.

The course develops an analytical understanding of the relationship between human-rights norms and the objectives, institutions, procedures, and operational mechanisms of the United Nations.

Particular attention is given to:

- The historical development of the United Nations.
- Human rights in the UN Charter.
- The Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
- The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.
- The International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights.
- UN human-rights agencies and mechanisms.
- Humanitarian crises and international responses.
- Group rights and collective dimensions of human rights.
- International courts and tribunals.
- The International Criminal Court.
- Humanitarian intervention and the Responsibility to Protect.
- State accountability and compliance with human-rights obligations.
- Career opportunities in the United Nations and its specialized agencies.

The UN identifies the promotion and protection of human rights as a core purpose and guiding principle of the organization. The Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights serves as the principal UN focal point for human-rights activities. [3][6]

---

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students should be able to:

1. Understand the historical foundations, objectives, principles, and institutional structure of the United Nations.
2. Examine the role of the United Nations in promoting and protecting human rights.
3. Analyze the relationship between human rights, the rule of law, development, peace, and security.
4. Understand the major human-rights instruments associated with the United Nations.
5. Examine the role of UN agencies, treaty bodies, special procedures, and other human-rights mechanisms.
6. Analyze the UN response to humanitarian crises and serious human-rights violations.
7. Understand the collective and group dimensions of human rights.
8. Critically assess international courts, tribunals, and accountability mechanisms.
9. Examine debates surrounding humanitarian intervention and the Responsibility to Protect.
10. Explore academic and professional opportunities connected with the United Nations and its specialized agencies.

## Course Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

### CO1: Explain the UN framework

### CO2: Analyze contemporary challenges

Establish conceptual and practical linkages between human-rights norms and the missions, institutions, procedures, and operational mechanisms of the United Nations.

### CO4: Evaluate accountability

Assess the strengths and limitations of international courts, tribunals, treaty mechanisms, humanitarian intervention, the Responsibility to Protect, and state accountability.

### CO5: Explore professional pathways

Explore career opportunities related to the United Nations and its specialized agencies in the fields of human rights, humanitarian governance, international development, peacebuilding, and global policy.

# Course Units

## Unit I: Introduction

### 1. Historical Background and Establishment of the United Nations

- International conditions preceding the establishment of the United Nations.
- The impact of the Second World War.
- The San Francisco Conference.
- Purposes and principles of the United Nations.
- The UN Charter and the contemporary international order.
- The relationship between peace, security, development, and human rights.

### 2. Human Rights in the UN Charter

- Human-rights references in the UN Charter.
- Human dignity and international responsibility.
- Sovereignty and international obligations.
- Non-discrimination and equality.
- Human rights as a principle of international cooperation.
- The relationship between human rights and international peace and security.

### 3. Human Rights, Rule of Law, and Development

- Meaning and significance of the rule of law.
- Human rights and democratic governance.
- Human rights and sustainable development.
- Social, political, and economic dimensions of development.
- Accountability and institutional responsibility.
- Participation, inclusion, and access to justice.

### 4. UN Human-Rights Instruments

- Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
- International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.
- International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights.
- Civil and political rights.
- Economic, social, and cultural rights.
- Universality, indivisibility, interdependence, and interrelatedness of rights.

The UDHR was adopted by the UN General Assembly in 1948. Together with the ICCPR and ICESCR, it forms the core of what is commonly called the International Bill of Human Rights. [2][3]

## Unit II: UN Human-Rights Agencies and Mechanisms

### 1. United Nations Human Rights Council

- Establishment and institutional position.
- Structure and membership.
- Functions and responsibilities.
- Universal Periodic Review.
- Special procedures.
- Commissions of inquiry and fact-finding missions.
- Country situations and thematic mandates.
- Political debates and institutional limitations.

### 2. Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights

- Historical establishment of OHCHR.
- Mandate and institutional role.
- Human-rights monitoring.
- Technical assistance.
- Public reporting.
- Advocacy and awareness.
- Engagement with governments, national institutions, civil society, and other stakeholders.

OHCHR supports the Human Rights Council, treaty bodies, and other UN human-rights organs, while also undertaking field activities and providing human-rights expertise. [6][12]

### 3. UN Treaty Bodies

- State reporting procedures.
- Concluding observations.
- General comments and recommendations.
- Individual communications where applicable.
- Follow-up procedures.
- Relationship between treaty bodies and domestic institutions.

### 4. UN Response to Humanitarian Crises

- Humanitarian protection.
- Emergency relief and international cooperation.
- Protection of civilians.
- Refugee and internally displaced populations.
- Humanitarian access.
- Conflict-related human-rights violations.
- Humanitarian coordination.
- Challenges of neutrality, impartiality, and sovereignty.

## Classroom Activities

Students may be asked to:

- Analyze selected provisions of the UN Charter.
- Compare the UDHR, ICCPR, and ICESCR.
- Map the structure of the UN human-rights system.
- Examine a UN Human Rights Council resolution.
- Prepare a briefing on a humanitarian crisis.
- Debate humanitarian intervention and sovereignty.
- Conduct a mock Universal Periodic Review.
- Prepare a policy brief on state accountability.
- Analyze the role of international courts and tribunals.
- Explore career pathways in UN agencies and human-rights organizations.

# Suggested Assignments

## Assignment 1: UN Charter Analysis

**Task:** Examine the place of human rights within the purposes and principles of the UN Charter.

**Expected output:** Analytical essay or presentation.

## Assignment 2: Human-Rights Instrument Comparison

**Task:** Compare the UDHR, ICCPR, and ICESCR in terms of scope, rights protected, legal character, and implementation.

**Expected output:** Comparative matrix and written analysis.

## Assignment 3: UN Mechanism Case Study

**Task:** Select one UN human-rights mechanism and assess its structure, mandate, achievements, and limitations.

**Expected output:** Research paper or policy brief.

## Assignment 4: Humanitarian Crisis Briefing

**Task:** Analyze a humanitarian crisis and evaluate the response of the United Nations.

**Expected output:** Policy briefing supported by credible sources.

## Assignment 5: Responsibility to Protect Debate

**Task:** Examine whether the Responsibility to Protect strengthens or weakens international human-rights protection.

**Expected output:** Structured debate and reflective paper.

## Assignment 6: Career Exploration

**Task:** Identify career opportunities in the United Nations, specialized agencies, international NGOs, human-rights institutions, or humanitarian organizations.

**Expected output:** Career map and professional-development plan.
