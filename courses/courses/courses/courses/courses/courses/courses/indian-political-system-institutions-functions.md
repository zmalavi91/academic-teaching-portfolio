## Course Overview

This course develops a comprehensive understanding of the Indian political system through the study of constitutional institutions and governmental mechanisms at three interconnected levels:

- Union government.
- State government.
- Grassroots and local government.

The course begins with the basic philosophy and salient features of the Constitution of India. It then examines the organization and functioning of the Union and State governments, including their legislatures, executives, and judiciaries.

The course also introduces students to local self-government, including:

- Panchayati Raj Institutions.
- Gram Panchayats.
- Panchayat Samitis.
- Zilla Parishads.
- Urban local bodies.
- Municipal governance.
- Grassroots participation and decentralization.

The course concludes with an examination of constitutional bodies, particularly:

- Comptroller and Auditor General of India.
- Attorney General for India.

The Ministry of Panchayati Raj describes India’s federal structure as having Union, State, and local levels of government, while Panchayati Raj Institutions form an important part of decentralized rural governance. [71][75]

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment institutionalized Panchayati Raj Institutions and provided for village, intermediate, and district-level Panchayats. Article 243G recognizes Panchayats as institutions of local self-government with responsibilities relating to economic development and social justice. [72][77]

# Course Units

## Unit I: The Structure of Union Government

## 1. Union Legislature

The Union Legislature is Parliament, consisting of:

- The President.
- The Rajya Sabha.
- The Lok Sabha.

Parliament performs legislative, representative, deliberative, financial, and accountability functions.

### Composition

Students examine:

- Composition of the Lok Sabha.
- Composition of the Rajya Sabha.
- Representation of citizens.
- Representation of States and Union Territories.
- Parliamentary leadership.
- Presiding officers.
- Parliamentary committees.
- Opposition parties.
- Qualifications and disqualifications.

### Functions of Parliament

Parliament:

- Makes laws.
- Represents political interests.
- Debates national issues.
- Controls public finance.
- Approves taxation and expenditure.
- Holds the executive accountable.
- Participates in constitutional amendments.
- Examines public policy.
- Provides a forum for democratic deliberation.

### Law-Making Procedure

Students study:

- Introduction of bills.
- First reading.
- Second reading.
- Committee stage.
- Clause-by-clause consideration.
- Debate and voting.
- Third reading.
- Passage by both Houses.
- Presidential assent.
- Publication and implementation.

### Parliamentary Accountability

Important mechanisms include:

- Question Hour.
- Zero Hour.
- Motions.
- Parliamentary debates.
- Calling attention procedures.
- Parliamentary committees.
- No-confidence motions.
- Budgetary control.
- Public Accounts Committee.
- Departmentally related standing committees.

## 2. Union Executive

The Union Executive includes:

- President.
- Vice-President.
- Prime Minister.
- Council of Ministers.
- Cabinet.
- Union ministries and departments.

### President

The President is the constitutional head of the Union.

Topics include:

- Electoral procedure.
- Constitutional position.
- Executive powers.
- Legislative powers.
- Financial powers.
- Emergency powers.
- Pardoning powers.
- Ordinance-making power.
- Relationship with the Council of Ministers.

### Council of Ministers

The Council of Ministers:

- Advises the President.
- Implements laws and policies.
- Exercises political responsibility.
- Coordinates Union administration.
- Is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
- Supports the Prime Minister in governance.

### Prime Minister

The Prime Minister:

- Leads the Union Government.
- Advises the President.
- Selects and coordinates ministers.
- Presides over Cabinet meetings.
- Determines policy priorities.
- Leads the government in Parliament.
- Coordinates Union-State relations.
- Represents India in national and international affairs.

### Cabinet

The Cabinet is the central decision-making body of the Union Executive.

Functions include:

- Formulating major policies.
- Coordinating ministries.
- Preparing legislative priorities.
- Managing public finance.
- Making national-security decisions.
- Guiding foreign policy.
- Responding to emergencies.
- Supervising public administration.

## 3. Union Judiciary

The Supreme Court is the apex judicial institution of India.

### Composition

Students examine:

- Chief Justice of India.
- Other judges.
- Appointment and qualifications.
- Tenure and removal.
- Judicial independence.
- Administrative structure.

### Jurisdiction

The Supreme Court exercises:

- Original jurisdiction.
- Appellate jurisdiction.
- Advisory jurisdiction.
- Writ jurisdiction.
- Special-leave jurisdiction.
- Review jurisdiction.
- Constitutional jurisdiction.

### Judicial Review

Judicial review allows courts to examine:

- Constitutional validity of laws.
- Executive and administrative action.
- Fundamental-rights violations.
- Constitutional amendments within established limits.
- Disputes between constitutional institutions.
- Federal disputes.

### Constitutional Role

The Supreme Court:

- Interprets the Constitution.
- Protects Fundamental Rights.
- Resolves institutional conflicts.
- Maintains constitutional supremacy.
- Develops constitutional principles.
- Protects the rule of law.

## Unit II: The Structure of State Government

## 1. State Legislature

A State Legislature may consist of:

- Governor.
- Legislative Assembly.
- Legislative Council, where established.

### Composition

Students examine:

- Legislative Assembly.
- Legislative Council.
- Members and constituencies.
- Presiding officers.
- Parliamentary parties.
- Legislative committees.
- Bicameral and unicameral State Legislatures.

### Functions

The State Legislature:

- Makes laws within State constitutional competence.
- Represents citizens and territorial interests.
- Controls State finances.
- Approves the State Budget.
- Holds the State Executive accountable.
- Debates public policy.
- Participates in legislative and constitutional processes.
- Examines government performance.

### Legislative Procedure

Topics include:

- Introduction of bills.
- First and second readings.
- Committee review.
- Debate and voting.
- Passage through legislative chambers.
- Governor’s assent.
- Reservation of bills for the President.
- Money bills.
- Ordinance replacement.

## 2. Governor

The Governor is the constitutional head of the State.

### Powers and Position

Students examine:

- Appointment.
- Term and conditions of office.
- Executive authority.
- Legislative authority.
- Financial responsibilities.
- Pardoning powers within constitutional limits.
- Ordinance-making power.
- Relationship with the Chief Minister.
- Role during political uncertainty.
- Discretionary powers.

### Executive Powers

The Governor:

- Appoints the Chief Minister.
- Appoints ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- Receives information from the Chief Minister.
- Appoints certain constitutional officials according to applicable provisions.
- Exercises formal executive authority.
- Performs constitutional functions in relation to State administration.

### Legislative Powers

The Governor:

- Summons and prorogues the State Legislature.
- Addresses the Legislature.
- Gives assent to bills.
- Returns certain bills for reconsideration.
- Reserves certain bills for Presidential consideration.
- Promulgates ordinances under constitutional conditions.

## 3. Chief Minister and Council of Ministers

### Chief Minister

The Chief Minister:

- Leads the State Government.
- Forms and coordinates the Council of Ministers.
- Advises the Governor.
- Allocates portfolios.
- Leads State policy.
- Represents the State in Union-State relations.
- Maintains the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.

### State Council of Ministers

The State Council of Ministers:

- Advises the Governor.
- Implements State laws and policies.
- Coordinates administration.
- Is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
- Develops public policy.
- Manages State departments.

## Unit III: Local Self-Government

## 1. Panchayati Raj Institutions

Panchayati Raj Institutions are rural local-government institutions designed to support grassroots participation, local planning, and decentralized governance.

The three-tier rural structure generally includes:

- Gram Panchayat at the village level.
- Panchayat Samiti or Block Panchayat at the intermediate level.
- Zilla Parishad at the district level.

The Ministry of Panchayati Raj describes Panchayati Raj as part of India’s three-tier governmental structure and works on decentralization and local governance. [71][75]

### Constitutional Foundation

Students examine:

- The 73rd Constitutional Amendment.
- Part IX of the Constitution.
- Article 243 and related provisions.
- Eleventh Schedule.
- Gram Sabha.
- Reservation of seats.
- State Election Commissions.
- State Finance Commissions.
- Local planning and development.

### Gram Sabha

The Gram Sabha:

- Provides a forum for village-level participation.
- Discusses local development priorities.
- Promotes public accountability.
- Supports social audit and transparency.
- Encourages citizen involvement in local governance.

### Gram Panchayat

The Gram Panchayat:

- Functions at the village level.
- Implements local development programmes.
- Provides civic and public services.
- Maintains local records.
- Supports sanitation, roads, water, and community welfare.
- Encourages village-level participation.

### Panchayat Samiti

The Panchayat Samiti:

- Operates at the intermediate or block level.
- Coordinates Gram Panchayats.
- Supports development planning.
- Connects village and district administration.
- Implements programmes across a wider territorial area.

### Zilla Parishad

The Zilla Parishad:

- Functions at the district level.
- Coordinates district-level planning.
- Integrates local development programmes.
- Supervises or supports intermediate-level institutions.
- Connects local governance with district administration.

### Functions of Panchayati Raj Institutions

Functions may include:

- Local development planning.
- Rural infrastructure.
- Drinking water.
- Sanitation.
- Public health.
- Primary education.
- Poverty reduction.
- Social welfare.
- Agricultural development.
- Women’s empowerment.
- Community participation.
- Social justice.

Article 243G recognizes Panchayats as institutions of local self-government and provides for responsibilities related to economic development and social justice, subject to the constitutional and legal framework. [72]

## 2. Urban Local Bodies

Urban local government institutions include:

- Nagar Panchayats.
- Municipal Councils.
- Municipal Corporations.

### Nagar Panchayat

A Nagar Panchayat generally serves a transitional area moving from rural to urban conditions.

### Municipal Council

A Municipal Council governs a smaller urban area.

### Municipal Corporation

A Municipal Corporation governs a larger urban area.

### Functions of Urban Local Bodies

Urban local bodies may be responsible for:

- Urban planning.
- Land-use regulation.
- Roads and public transport.
- Water supply.
- Sanitation.
- Waste management.
- Public health.
- Fire services.
- Slum improvement.
- Urban poverty reduction.
- Environmental protection.
- Local taxation.
- Public spaces and community facilities.

### Constitutional Foundation

Students examine:

- 74th Constitutional Amendment.
- Part IX-A.
- Article 243Q.
- Twelfth Schedule.
- Municipal elections.
- Reservation.
- State Election Commission.
- State Finance Commission.
- Urban planning and accountability.

The constitutional framework provides for the constitution of Municipalities under Article 243Q. [74]

## 3. Significance of Local Self-Government

Local self-government promotes:

- Democratic decentralization.
- Participation.
- Representation.
- Accountability.
- Local knowledge.
- Social inclusion.
- Women’s political participation.
- Community development.
- Responsive public services.
- Grassroots leadership.
