## Course Overview

This course familiarizes students with the main currents, paradigms, and systems of thought in contemporary Political Science.

It examines the dominant ideologies that influence political discourse, social movements, constitutional arrangements, public policy, and the behaviour of governments across the world.

The course has both theoretical and applied dimensions. It introduces students to the concepts, assumptions, values, historical development, and institutional implications of major ideologies, while also encouraging them to apply ideological frameworks to contemporary political issues.

The course is particularly relevant for students preparing for:

- UGC-NET.
- JRF examinations.
- Civil Services examinations.
- State-level eligibility tests.
- Postgraduate study in Political Science.
- Research in Political Theory and Comparative Politics.
- Policy analysis and public affairs.

The course covers:

- The meaning, nature, and scope of ideology.
- The relationship between ideology and Political Theory.
- The End of Ideology debate.
- Liberalism.
- Marxism.
- Feminism.
- Nationalism.
- Ecologism.
- Indian political thought, with special attention to Gandhism and B. R. Ambedkar.

Liberalism is examined as a political tradition concerned with liberty, individual agency, political authority, and the justification of government. [84][87]

Feminism is studied both as an intellectual tradition and as a political movement concerned with understanding and ending gender-based oppression. [85][86]

Ecological and feminist approaches are also connected through questions concerning power, domination, environmental justice, social reproduction, and the relationship between human communities and the natural world. [83]

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students should be able to:

1. Understand the meaning, nature, scope, and political significance of ideology.
2. Examine the relationship between ideology and Political Theory.
3. Analyze the End of Ideology debate from Liberal and Marxist perspectives.
4. Understand the historical origins and development of liberalism.
5. Examine classical liberalism, positive liberalism, and neoliberalism.
6. Understand the main concepts of Marxism.
7. Analyze dialectical materialism, historical materialism, class struggle, revolution, and communism.
8. Examine the basic ideas of Neo-Marxism.
9. Understand feminism, nationalism, and ecologism as political ideologies.
10. Analyze civic and ethnic forms of nationalism.
11. Examine deep ecology and sustainable development.
12. Understand Indian political ideologies, particularly Gandhism and the political thought of B. R. Ambedkar.
13. Apply ideological frameworks to contemporary political and policy questions.
14. Develop conceptual skills relevant to UGC-NET, JRF, Civil Services, and other competitive examinations.

## Course Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

### CO1: Explain ideology

Explain the meaning, nature, scope, functions, and political relevance of ideology.

### CO2: Evaluate the End of Ideology debate

Analyze the End of Ideology debate from Liberal, Marxist, and critical perspectives.

### CO3: Understand liberalism

Describe the origins, development, core principles, and varieties of liberalism.

### CO4: Understand Marxism

Explain the basic concepts of Marxism, including materialism, class struggle, revolution, proletarian dictatorship, communism, and Neo-Marxism.

### CO5: Analyze contemporary ideologies

Examine feminism, nationalism, ecologism, multiculturalism, and related ideological frameworks.

### CO6: Connect ideology with public policy

Assess how political ideologies influence governmental institutions, economic policy, social policy, rights, citizenship, and international affairs.

### CO7: Understand Indian political thought

Explain major ideas associated with Gandhism and the political thought of B. R. Ambedkar.

### CO8: Apply concepts for examinations and research

Use ideological concepts accurately in academic writing, competitive examinations, research essays, debates, and policy analysis.
