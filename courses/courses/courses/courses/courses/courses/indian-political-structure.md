## Course Overview

This course develops a detailed understanding of the governmental mechanisms established under the Constitution of India.

It examines the composition, powers, functions, jurisdiction, and constitutional relationships of important institutions at the Union and State levels. Particular attention is given to the legislature, executive, judiciary, constitutional authorities, and processes of accountability.

The course enables students to understand:

- The structure and functions of Parliament.
- The legislative process at the Union level.
- Ordinary bills and Constitution Amendment Bills.
- The Union Budget procedure.
- The constitutional position of the President.
- The role of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers.
- The relationship between the Cabinet and Parliament.
- The composition and jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
- Judicial review, the Basic Structure Doctrine, and Public Interest Litigation.
- The constitutional position of the Governor.
- The role of the Chief Minister and State executive.
- The composition and functioning of State Legislatures.
- The relationship between Union and State institutions.
- Constitutional accountability and institutional checks.

The Constitution of India establishes Parliament as consisting of the President and two Houses: the Council of States, or Rajya Sabha, and the House of the People, or Lok Sabha. [58][65]

The Supreme Court of India exercises original, appellate, advisory, and constitutional jurisdictions. Its official materials describe, among other functions, disputes involving the Union and States and the power to grant special leave to appeal under Article 136. [56][66]

# Course Units

## Unit I: Union Legislature

## 1. Parliament of India

Parliament is the central legislative institution of the Union.

It consists of:

- The President.
- The Rajya Sabha.
- The Lok Sabha.

Parliament performs legislative, deliberative, representative, financial, and accountability functions.

## 2. Lok Sabha: Composition and Functions

The Lok Sabha is the House of the People.

### Composition

Topics include:

- Direct election of representatives.
- Territorial constituencies.
- Representation of the people.
- Duration of the House.
- Qualifications and disqualifications.
- Speaker and Deputy Speaker.
- Parliamentary parties and opposition.
- Committees of the House.

### Functions

The Lok Sabha:

- Makes laws.
- Represents citizens.
- Controls public finance.
- Participates in the budget process.
- Holds the Council of Ministers accountable.
- Debates national issues.
- Participates in constitutional amendments.
- Exercises political control through questions, motions, and debates.
- Plays a central role in determining whether the government enjoys legislative confidence.

## 3. Rajya Sabha: Composition and Functions

The Rajya Sabha is the Council of States.

### Composition

Topics include:

- Representation of States and Union Territories.
- Elected and nominated members.
- Duration and continuity.
- Chairman and Deputy Chairman.
- Parliamentary committees.
- Representation of territorial interests.

### Functions

The Rajya Sabha:

- Participates in law-making.
- Represents the States.
- Reviews and debates legislation.
- Participates in the budgetary process.
- Exercises legislative scrutiny.
- Participates in constitutional amendments.
- Performs special functions relating to federal representation.
- Provides continuity because it is not subject to dissolution in the same way as the Lok Sabha.

## 4. Procedure over Ordinary Bills

Students examine:

- Introduction of an ordinary bill.
- First reading.
- Publication and circulation.
- Committee stage.
- Second reading.
- Detailed consideration.
- Clause-by-clause discussion.
- Third reading.
- Transmission between Houses.
- Presidential assent.
- Joint sitting in relevant circumstances.
- Role of parliamentary committees.

## 5. Procedure over Constitution Amendment Bills

Students examine:

- Constitutional authority to amend the Constitution.
- Introduction of a Constitution Amendment Bill.
- Special majority requirements.
- Passage by both Houses.
- State ratification where constitutionally required.
- Presidential assent.
- Limits imposed by the Basic Structure Doctrine.
- Relationship between constitutional change and constitutional identity.

## 6. Budget Procedure

The Union Budget process may be studied through:

- Presentation of the Annual Financial Statement.
- Demands for grants.
- General discussion.
- Departmentally related standing committees.
- Voting on demands for grants.
- Appropriation Bill.
- Finance Bill.
- Parliamentary control over expenditure.
- Public Accounts Committee.
- Financial accountability.

## Unit I: Suggested Discussion Questions

- Why does Parliament include the President?
- How does the Rajya Sabha represent federal interests?
- What is the significance of parliamentary committees?
- Why are Constitution Amendment Bills subject to special procedures?
- How does the budget process create legislative control over public finance?
- What is the relationship between parliamentary majority and executive stability?

## Unit II: Union Executive

## 1. The President of India

The President is the constitutional head of the Union Executive.

### Electoral Procedure

Students examine:

- Indirect election.
- Electoral college.
- Representation of States and Union Territories.
- Value of votes.
- Proportional representation.
- Single transferable vote.
- Constitutional election procedure.

### Conditions of Office

Topics include:

- Term of office.
- Qualifications.
- Oath or affirmation.
- Conditions of office.
- Impeachment.
- Vacancy and succession.
- Constitutional privileges and limitations.

### Executive Powers

The President:

- Appoints the Prime Minister.
- Appoints other ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister.
- Appoints constitutional authorities according to constitutional procedures.
- Exercises executive authority in the name of the Union.
- Represents the Union in constitutional and formal matters.
- Performs functions related to administration and appointments.

### Legislative Powers

The President:

- Summons and prorogues Parliament.
- Addresses Parliament.
- Gives assent to bills.
- May return certain bills for reconsideration.
- Promulgates ordinances under constitutional conditions.
- Nominates members where constitutionally provided.
- Receives and acts on parliamentary procedures.

### Emergency Powers

Students examine:

- National Emergency.
- President’s Rule in a State.
- Financial Emergency.
- Constitutional safeguards.
- Parliamentary approval.
- Effects on federalism and fundamental rights.
- Judicial review of emergency action.

### Discretionary Powers

The President may exercise limited discretion in constitutionally significant situations, including:

- Appointment of a Prime Minister when no party or coalition has a clear majority.
- Decisions concerning government formation.
- Return of advice for reconsideration where constitutionally permitted.
- Constitutional action during political uncertainty.

The exercise of discretion must be examined in relation to constitutional conventions, parliamentary responsibility, and democratic accountability.

## 2. Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

### Prime Minister

The Prime Minister is the head of government and the principal coordinator of the Union Executive.

Functions include:

- Forming the Council of Ministers.
- Allocating portfolios.
- Leading Cabinet meetings.
- Coordinating government policy.
- Advising the President.
- Providing leadership in Parliament.
- Coordinating Union-State relations.
- Representing the government nationally and internationally.
- Guiding legislative and administrative priorities.

### Council of Ministers

The Council of Ministers:

- Advises the President.
- Exercises political responsibility for executive policy.
- Implements legislation.
- Formulates public policy.
- Remains collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
- Coordinates administration across ministries.

### Cabinet

The Cabinet is the central decision-making body within the Council of Ministers.

Functions include:

- Determining major policy.
- Coordinating ministries.
- Taking decisions on national security and foreign policy.
- Preparing legislative priorities.
- Managing public finance.
- Responding to emergencies.
- Supervising administration.

## Unit II: Suggested Discussion Questions

- What is the constitutional relationship between the President and Prime Minister?
- Why is the President generally described as the formal or constitutional executive?
- What is collective responsibility?
- How does the Cabinet differ from the broader Council of Ministers?
- What are the constitutional and political limits on executive power?
- How do emergency powers affect federalism and democratic rights?

## Unit III: Apex Judiciary

## 1. Supreme Court of India

The Supreme Court is the apex judicial institution of India.

### Composition

Topics include:

- Chief Justice of India.
- Other judges of the Supreme Court.
- Appointment.
- Qualifications.
- Tenure.
- Removal.
- Independence of the judiciary.
- Administrative organization.
- Constitutional position.

The Supreme Court’s official website states that it consists of the Chief Justice of India and not more than 33 other judges appointed by the President of India. [66]

### Original Jurisdiction

The Supreme Court has original jurisdiction in constitutionally specified disputes, including certain disputes between:

- Government of India and one or more States.
- Government of India and one or more States on one side and other States on the other.
- Two or more States.

Such disputes must involve a legal right whose existence or extent is in question. [56]

### Appellate Jurisdiction

The Supreme Court hears appeals from:

- High Courts.
- Tribunals.
- Other courts where constitutionally or legally permitted.
- Cases involving substantial constitutional or legal questions.
- Matters admitted through special leave to appeal under Article 136.

### Advisory Jurisdiction

The President may refer questions of law or fact of public importance for the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court in accordance with the Constitution.

### Writ Jurisdiction

The Supreme Court may issue constitutional writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Important writs include:

- Habeas corpus.
- Mandamus.
- Prohibition.
- Certiorari.
- Quo warranto.

### Judicial Review

Judicial review enables the courts to examine:

- Constitutional validity of legislation.
- Executive action.
- Administrative decisions.
- Restrictions on fundamental rights.
- Constitutional amendments within established limits.
- Federal disputes and institutional conflicts.

## 2. High Courts

High Courts are the highest judicial institutions at the State level.

Topics include:

- Composition.
- Appointment of judges.
- Jurisdiction.
- Writ powers.
- Appeals.
- Superintendence over subordinate courts.
- Judicial review.
- Relationship with the Supreme Court.
- Role in protecting constitutional and legal rights.

## 3. Doctrine of Basic Structure

The Basic Structure Doctrine establishes that Parliament’s power to amend the Constitution is not unlimited.

Students examine:

- Constitutional amendment power.
- Judicial interpretation.
- Limits on amendment.
- Constitutional identity.
- Supremacy of the Constitution.
- Rule of law.
- Judicial independence.
- Secularism.
- Federalism.
- Democracy.
- Separation of powers.
- Fundamental rights.
- Judicial review.

The doctrine is studied as a constitutional mechanism for balancing democratic amendment with the preservation of foundational constitutional principles.

## 4. Public Interest Litigation

Public Interest Litigation enables courts to address issues affecting public interest, especially where disadvantaged or marginalized groups may face barriers to accessing justice.

Topics include:

- Relaxed rules of standing.
- Access to justice.
- Rights of vulnerable communities.
- Social action litigation.
- Judicial activism.
- Continuing mandamus.
- Public accountability.
- PIL and environmental protection.
- PIL and human rights.
- Risks of judicial overreach.

## Unit III: Suggested Discussion Questions

- Why is judicial independence important?
- How does the Supreme Court balance rights and institutional limits?
- What is the relationship between judicial review and parliamentary sovereignty?
- Why is the Basic Structure Doctrine significant?
- Has Public Interest Litigation expanded access to justice?
- What are the risks of judicial overreach?
- How do Supreme Court and High Court jurisdictions differ?

## Unit IV: State Government

## 1. The Governor

The Governor is the constitutional head of the State Executive.

### Appointment and Removal

Students examine:

- Appointment by the President.
- Constitutional position.
- Term of office.
- Removal and transfer.
- Relationship with the Union Executive.
- Constitutional conventions.
- Role during political uncertainty.

### Conditions of Office

Topics include:

- Qualifications.
- Oath or affirmation.
- Term.
- Conditions of office.
- Constitutional immunity.
- Relationship with the State Council of Ministers.

### Executive Powers

The Governor:

- Appoints the Chief Minister.
- Appoints other ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- Appoints constitutional authorities according to constitutional provisions.
- Exercises executive authority in the name of the State.
- Receives information from the Chief Minister.
- Performs formal constitutional functions.

### Legislative Powers

The Governor:

- Summons and prorogues the State Legislature.
- Addresses the State Legislature.
- Gives assent to State bills.
- May return certain bills for reconsideration.
- Reserves certain bills for consideration by the President.
- Promulgates ordinances under constitutional conditions.
- Performs constitutionally recognized legislative functions.

### Discretionary Powers

The Governor may exercise limited discretion in situations such as:

- Appointment of a Chief Minister when no party has a clear majority.
- Determining whether a ministry commands legislative confidence.
- Reserving certain bills for the President.
- Reporting constitutional breakdown where constitutionally relevant.
- Performing special responsibilities where provided by the Constitution.

The exercise of discretion must be analyzed in relation to federalism, democratic accountability, constitutional conventions, and judicial review.

## 2. Chief Minister and State Council of Ministers

The Chief Minister:

- Leads the State Government.
- Forms the Council of Ministers.
- Advises the Governor.
- Coordinates departments.
- Leads legislative and policy priorities.
- Represents the State in Union-State relations.
- Maintains the confidence of the State Legislative Assembly.

The State Council of Ministers:

- Advises the Governor.
- Implements State laws and policies.
- Is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
- Coordinates State administration.
- Develops policies relating to subjects within State and concurrent fields.

## 3. State Legislature

### Composition

A State Legislature may consist of:

- Governor.
- Legislative Assembly.
- Legislative Council in States with a bicameral legislature.

### Legislative Assembly

The Legislative Assembly:

- Represents the people of the State.
- Makes laws within constitutional competence.
- Controls State finances.
- Holds the Council of Ministers accountable.
- Debates State policies.
- Participates in constitutional and electoral processes.

### Legislative Council

Where established, the Legislative Council:

- Provides a second chamber.
- Reviews legislation.
- Represents different interests and constituencies.
- Performs deliberative and revising functions.
- Contributes to legislative scrutiny.

### Powers and Functions

The State Legislature:

- Makes laws on subjects within its legislative competence.
- Passes the State Budget.
- Approves taxation and expenditure.
- Exercises legislative oversight.
- Participates in constitutional amendment procedures where required.
- Discusses public policy and local concerns.
- Holds the executive accountable.

### State Legislative Process

Topics include:

- Introduction of bills.
- First reading.
- Committee examination.
- Debate and consideration.
- Passage by the legislature.
- Governor’s assent.
- Reservation for Presidential consideration.
- Ordinance-making and legislative replacement.
- Money bills and financial procedure.

- How does a State Legislative Assembly hold the executive accountable?
- What is the purpose of a Legislative Council?
- How should gubernatorial discretion be exercised?
- What are the main constitutional issues in Union-State relations?

# Constitutional Accountability Institutions

Although this course focuses primarily on the Union Legislature, Union Executive, Apex Judiciary, and State Government, students should also understand the broader constitutional accountability structure.

## Comptroller and Auditor General

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India is a constitutional authority concerned with public financial accountability.

Key themes include:

- Audit of Union and State accounts.
- Examination of public expenditure.
- Reporting to constitutional authorities.
- Legislative scrutiny of audit findings.
- Financial accountability.
- Public resource management.
- Relationship with the Public Accounts Committee.

The CAG’s constitutional provisions derive primarily from Articles 148 to 151, and its duties include responsibilities concerning the accounts of the Union and States. [57][59]

## Union Public Service Commission

The Union Public Service Commission is a constitutional public-service institution.

Key themes include:

- Recruitment to public services.
- Competitive examinations.
- Consultation on appointments and service matters.
- Merit-based public administration.
- Institutional independence.
- Professionalization of the civil services.
- Constitutional accountability.

## Why These Institutions Matter

These institutions contribute to:

- Administrative integrity.
- Merit-based recruitment.
- Financial accountability.
- Constitutional governance.
- Legislative oversight.
- Public trust.
- Responsible administration.
