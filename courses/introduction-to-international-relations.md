# Introduction to International Relations

## Course Overview

This undergraduate course introduces students to the foundations of International Relations, including states, non-state actors, power, conflict, cooperation, multilateralism, and global governance.

## Core Themes

- International Relations theory.
- State and non-state actors.
- Power and national interest.
- International organizations.
- Global governance.
- Conflict and cooperation.
- Human rights in international politics.

## Teaching Methods

- Case studies.
- Socratic questioning.
- Student-led discussions.
- Research-based assignments.
- Contemporary geopolitical analysis.

## Learning Outcomes

Students should be able to:

1. Explain foundational concepts in International Relations.
2. Compare major approaches to world politics.
3. Analyze international institutions and global governance.
4. Apply theoretical concepts to contemporary cases.
5. Prepare evidence-based analytical work.

## Assessment and Student Engagement

Assessment may include classroom participation, written assignments, research projects, presentations, and continuous internal evaluation.

Students are encouraged to connect theoretical concepts with contemporary international events, treaties, institutions, conflicts, and human-rights questions.
